# Wall Display

A self-hosted smart wall calendar, similar to a Skylight calendar but running on your own server. It's a Nuxt app on the home server, a Chromium kiosk on a Raspberry Pi, and Home Assistant integration over MQTT.

- [Architecture](docs/architecture.md)
- [Home Assistant](docs/home-assistant.md)
- [Implementation plan](docs/implementation-plan.md)

## Development

The app lives in `calendar-app/`. It is a standalone pnpm project.

Prerequisites:

- Node 24 (see `.node-version`)
- pnpm, via corepack: `corepack enable`

```sh
cd calendar-app
pnpm install
pnpm dev
```

The dev server is at <http://localhost:3000>. Pages and endpoints so far:

- `/display`: the wall display. A 2560×1440 stage (or 1440×2560 on a portrait display, for a view that has a portrait layout: the night view, the agenda, the photo frame, the month, the week, the week by part of day, the week with a photo and a custom layout, which is every view so far; a custom layout can also hold the day grid, a grid of day tiles with their events, and the star chart, a grid of cards with each person's stars) scaled to fit the window, showing the view the server chose (the week by part of day, `week_parts`, is seven day rows each split into a morning, an afternoon and an evening, the boundaries being the display's `day_parts`, 12 and 17 by default: all-day events in the row's head with the date and weather, and each timed event as a line in the part it starts in, four lines a cell in landscape and eight in portrait, with "+N more" beyond them; the week and photo view, `week_photo`, is a photo pane beside or above this week: on a landscape display the photo is a 1100 px pane on the left, with its right corners rounded, and the week fills the rest, and on a portrait display the photo is a 1000 px pane across the top, with its lower corners rounded, and the week fills the rest. The week there is the narrow seven-column layout under a short title (`26 Oct – 1 Nov`, with `This week`, `Next week`, `Last week` or the year beside it), and the photo pane has the photo frame's compact pill (a 96 px clock and the weather) without the next event, since the week shows it. The photo is rendered to the pane's shape, so a landscape display, whose pane is about 3:4, is sent the portrait rendition and a portrait display, whose pane is wider than it is tall, the landscape one, and it fills the pane. Photos rotate in it as in the photo frame, and Next photo skips them; the week view is seven day tiles for the week of today, the week pinned with `set_week`, or the week of a previewed date: all-day and multi-day events in a strip across the top, timed events as blocks on an hour scale with overlapping ones side by side (two at most on a portrait display, with a "+N" chip for the rest), and a "now" line. The hours drawn in full are the display's `week_hours`, 7 to 21 by default; earlier and later hours are folded into a thin band at each end that still lists what is in them; the custom view, `custom`, draws one of your layouts, which arrange these views and the photo frame into rows and panes, with a landscape and a portrait arrangement each: see Custom layouts, below) in the theme set for the display in config. `/display?id=hallway` picks a display; with no `id` it shows the first one in the config (or the last one it showed, if the server can't be reached). A display is portrait when its `orientation` says so, or with `auto` (the default) when the window is taller than it is wide, and `?orientation=auto|landscape|portrait` overrides that for one device. It keeps the last-known state and events in `localStorage`, so a reload while the server is down still shows the wall, and a small "Offline" badge appears once the WebSocket has been down for 3 seconds. `brightness` dims it with a black overlay and `screen: off` makes it black. The `photo` view is the photo frame (and `week_photo` has the same photo and pill in its pane): one photo fullscreen, with a slow 1.5 second crossfade to the next. The wall asks `GET /api/photos/next?display=<id>&orientation=<landscape or portrait, as its stage is>` when it enters the view and again on every `photo.next` the server sends it, loads and decodes the new image first, and only then fades it in; a late answer to an older request is ignored, and if there is no photo (or the request fails) the current one stays, or with none at all a calm "No photos yet" message shows and it tries again every 15 seconds. A small dark, blurred pill in the top-left corner shows the clock (24-hour, display time zone), the current weather icon and temperature when there is weather, and a chip for the next event (left out in the `week_photo` pane; the first timed event that is on now or starts today or tomorrow, with its person's colour, the time and the title cut with an ellipsis; all-day events are left out, and with no such event the chip is left out). The pill is not themed by special days: the photo frame is never taken over. A message set with `set_overlay` shows as a large banner at the bottom of every view, the photo frame included, with its icon (a Lucide icon, named as the remote or Home Assistant gives it, such as `trash` or `mdi:bell`), under the dimming so screen off still blacks it out. It goes when it is cleared, and when its time is up even if the server can't be reached. It is installable on any device, not only the kiosk: "Add to Home Screen" (or Install, from a secure address: HTTPS or `localhost`) opens it fullscreen with the display's own icon, and a service worker keeps the app so an installed display opens offline with the state it last had. It keeps the screen awake, and in an ordinary browser tab shows a "Tap for fullscreen" pill. A device that opens `/display?id=hallway` mirrors that display (same view, dimming and screen off); for a demo that shouldn't follow the wall, add a display for it under `displays:`. Settings → Displays has an "Open on another device" link and QR code for each display
- `/remote`: the phone remote, built for a phone and following its light or dark setting. `/remote?id=hallway` picks a display; with no `id` it controls the first one in the config. From top to bottom it has the display's name with a connection pill (Connected, Reconnecting, or Offline after 10 seconds without the WebSocket), a Screen card with an on/off toggle (the rest fades to half opacity while the screen is off), tiles for Agenda, Month, Week and Day parts (the week by part of day view) in a 2×2 grid, with a full-width Night tile under them, and the Photo frame and Week + photo tiles side by side under that (half width each when both show), a Layouts row with a tile for each custom layout (tap one to put it on the wall; a layout with a photo pane is left out when no photo source is set up) and an Edit layouts button that opens the layouts editor on a full-height sheet, a Stars section (a line for each person who has a star chart, with their stars for today and a minus and a plus to take one away or give one; left out when nobody has a chart), a month picker (shown only in Month view, or a custom layout with a month: arrows and a dropdown covering 12 months either side, with "Back to this month" while another month is pinned), a week picker (shown only in the Week, Day parts and Week + photo views, or a custom layout with a week or day parts pane: arrows and a dropdown covering 52 weeks either side, with "Back to this week" while another week is pinned), a "Photos from" section (a segmented control for Immich, Google or Folder, which sets `photo_source`, with a source the server has not set up disabled and marked "Not set up", and a Next photo button that reads "Skipped" for two seconds; it is off while the wall is on none of the photo frame, the week and photo view and a custom layout with a photo pane), a Message section (what is on the wall now and when it ends, with Clear, and a form for a new message: text, an icon and how long it stays, from 30 minutes to until cleared) and a plain "More" section with brightness and Refresh calendars. The Photo frame and Week + photo tiles and "Photos from" are left out when the config has no photo source set up (no `photos` block, or one with neither `immich` nor `local`), because the wall would have nothing to show; a tile stays, lit, if Home Assistant has put the wall on its view anyway. The remote shows the source the wall really uses: a display left on Immich with only a folder set up is on the folder. It stays live over the same WebSocket as the display. If the config sets `remote.pin`, the page asks for the PIN once, keeps it in the phone's `localStorage` and sends it with every command. "Add to Home Screen" (Share menu on iOS, menu on Android) installs it as a full-screen app for that display, using the manifest and icon the server provides
- `/settings`: the admin area, with tabs for Calendars, Photos, Special days, Layouts, Stars and Displays. **Photos** is read-only: for each photo source that is set up it shows the name, Immich's mode and host (never the API key or the rest of the address) or the folder's path, how many photos are in the pool and how many are cached, when it last listed and the last error; the cache's size against `cache_max_mb` with a bar; and the interval and whether photos of the other shape are left out (`match_orientation`). "Refresh now" lists every source again (it needs the PIN, like any write). With no `photos` block the tab says the photo frame is off. **Layouts** is the editor for custom layouts (see Custom layouts, below). **Stars** switches each person's star chart on or off, sets whether it counts a day or a week and the goal, and corrects this week's counts (see Star chart, below). **Displays** shows each display's live state and connections, and edits its preferences: theme, month style, week start, how long a pinned month stays, the week view's hours, burn-in shifting, the night view's text and the orientation (auto, landscape or portrait). A card for every display edits the photo interval and the policies' thresholds (auto presence's wait and quiet hours, and auto brightness's night action, dark and light thresholds, step and smoothing). A saved change is stored in the database on top of `config.yaml` (which is never written) and takes effect at once, with no restart; each field marks a change and has Reset to go back to the config's value. Saving needs the PIN, like any write
- `/api/health`: returns `{ "status": "ok", "uptime": <seconds>, "db": "ok" }`, or a 503 with `{ "status": "error", "db": "error" }` if the database doesn't answer
- `GET /api/events?from=…&to=…`: the events that overlap the range `[from, to)`, each with its people (name, initial, colour) and its calendar's `icon` (an emoji, or null). `from` and `to` are a date such as `2026-10-01` (midnight at the start of that day in the display time zone) or an ISO 8601 time with `Z` or an offset. The range may be at most 366 days; anything else is a 400 with a short reason
- `GET /api/weather`: the weather the displays are showing, `{ "weather": { "current": { "condition", "temperature" }, "daily": [{ "date", "condition", "high", "low" }], "hourly": [{ "time", "condition", "temperature", "precipitation", "precipitation_probability" }], "temperature_unit", "updated_at" } }`, or `{ "weather": null }` when there is no `weather` block in the config or Home Assistant has not answered yet. It never holds the Home Assistant URL or token
- `POST /api/refresh`: syncs every calendar now and answers `{ "calendars": [{ "calendarId", "status": "updated" | "not-modified" | "error", "error"? }] }` once all have finished (it fetches the weather again too, which is not part of the answer). The error is a short message that never contains a feed URL. With `remote.pin` set in the config it needs the PIN in an `X-Wallcal-Pin` header (see the PIN note below)
- `GET /api/special-days` and `POST /api/special-days`: the special days (name, date, whether it repeats yearly or happens once, `scene_id`, `who`, `since_year`, and its own lead window, palette, greeting and takeover), and a way to add one, which answers 201 with the new day. `lead`, `takeover` and `palette` can be `null`, or left out, to use the scene's. `who` fills `{who}` in the greeting, and `since_year` (yearly days only, from 1900) fills `{years}` and `{nth}` and hides the day in that year and before. A bad body is a 400 with a short reason, and so is a `scene_id` that is not a scene, a `{…}` that is not one of `{who}`, `{years}` and `{nth}`, and a greeting the day can't fill (such as `{nth}` with no `since_year`), which names the day. Special days are stored in SQLite, not in the config
- `GET /api/special-days/resolved`: what the displays are drawing, `{ "special_days": { "today", "from", "to", "tiles", "current", "takeover", "tomorrow_art" } | null }`: the tile decorations for the months a display can show, today's special day and the one taking over, if any, and `tomorrow_art`, the art files tomorrow's answer will need (so the wall can load them in the evening). It is `null` until the server has worked them out. A change is sent to every display as `special_days.updated`
- `GET /api/special-days/preview?special_day=<id>` or `?scene=<id>`: the same answer as `/resolved`, worked out as if the date that day next falls on were today (a one-off day on its own date, even if that has passed). A scene is shown through the first day that uses it, or as a one-off day today with its placeholders as typed if none does. It covers that date's month grid and the takeover is that day's own. Exactly one of the two parameters is needed (otherwise 400), and an unknown day or scene is a 404. It is what the wall fetches during `preview_special_day`, and needs no PIN
- `GET /api/special-days/:id`, `PUT /api/special-days/:id` and `DELETE /api/special-days/:id`: read, replace or delete one special day, or a 404 for an unknown id. Delete answers 204. With `remote.pin` set, every write here needs the `X-Wallcal-Pin` header, as `POST /api/refresh` does
- `GET /api/special-day-scenes` and `POST /api/special-day-scenes`: the scenes, by name, and a way to add one (201). A scene is a look that days share: `lead` (and `lead_days`), `takeover`, `greeting` and `palette`, plus the art file names, which an edit never changes. A scene is a whole look, so none of those is `null` (`lead_days` apart, which is set only when the lead is `days`)
- `GET /api/special-day-scenes/:id`, `PUT /api/special-day-scenes/:id` and `DELETE /api/special-day-scenes/:id`: read, replace or delete one scene, or a 404 for an unknown id. A `PUT` with a greeting that some day on the scene couldn't fill is a 400 naming every such day (`data.days` is their `id` and `name`). Delete answers 204. A scene in use is a 409 that names its days, unless the request has `?detach=true`, which first gives each day the scene's values for the parts of the look it left empty, so the wall doesn't change. Editing a scene changes the wall for the days using it, and is sent as `special_days.updated`
- `POST /api/special-days/:id/duplicate` and `POST /api/special-day-scenes/:id/duplicate`: copy a day or a scene as "Copy of …", art files included, and answer 201 with the copy
- `POST /api/special-days/:id/save-as-scene`: makes a scene from the look the day has and moves the day onto it, clearing its own look. The body is optional: `{ "name": "…" }` names the scene, which is otherwise named after the day. Answers 201 with `{ "scene", "day" }`. The day's `updated_at` is not changed
- `POST` and `DELETE` on `/api/special-days/:id/art/:slot` (`tile`, `full`, `full-portrait`, `backdrop`, `backdrop-portrait`, `extra` or `extra-portrait`) and `/api/special-day-scenes/:id/art/:slot` (`tile`, `full`, `full-portrait`, `backdrop` or `backdrop-portrait`; scenes have no `extra` or `extra-portrait`): set or clear one piece of art. `POST` is `multipart/form-data` with the picture in a field named `file`, and answers 200 with the day or scene. `DELETE` clears the slot, so a day goes back to its scene's art, and also answers 200 with the row. Tile art is PNG, WebP or SVG up to 2 MB, fitted inside 512×512. Full art is any image the server can read (JPEG, PNG, WebP, GIF, AVIF, TIFF, SVG and so on) up to 20 MB, cropped to fill 2560×1440. Extra art is PNG, WebP or SVG with a transparent background up to 20 MB, cropped the same way as full art so the two line up, and an opaque image is refused. The portrait slots follow the rules of their landscape twins but are cropped to fill 1440×2560, for a portrait display. The type is read from the file itself, not from its name or `Content-Type`. SVGs are cleaned, and one with a script, an event handler, a `foreignObject`, an external link or an embedded `data:` image is refused rather than stored. The errors are 400 for a missing `file` field, a damaged image or a refused SVG, 413 for a file over the limit, 415 for the wrong kind of file, 411 for a request with no `Content-Length` and 404 for an unknown day, scene or slot. Files are named after a hash of what is stored, kept in `special-days/` in the data directory, and removed once no day or scene uses them. With `remote.pin` set, every write here needs the `X-Wallcal-Pin` header
- `GET /special-days/:name`: an art file, by the name a day or scene gives in `tile_art`, `full_art`, `full_art_portrait`, `backdrop_art`, `backdrop_art_portrait`, `extra_art` or `extra_art_portrait`. It is `image/webp` or `image/svg+xml` with `Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable` and `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`, and SVGs also have `Content-Security-Policy: default-src 'none'; style-src 'unsafe-inline'`. A name of any other shape, or a file that isn't there, is a 404. It is not under `/api` and needs no PIN, because the displays load it as an ordinary image
- `GET /api/layouts` and `POST /api/layouts`: the custom layouts, by name, and a way to add one (201). A layout is `{ id, name, landscape, portrait, created_at, updated_at }`; the `id` is a slug of the name. The body of a POST is `{ name, landscape, portrait }`, where each arrangement is `{ rows: [{ size, panes: [{ component, size }] }] }`. The `component` is `month`, `week`, `week_parts`, `agenda` or `photo`, each at most once in an arrangement and the same set in both; there are 1 to 4 rows of 1 to 4 panes, and a `size` is a whole number from 1 to 4. A bad body is a 400 naming the field, and a name another layout has (in any case) is a 409.
- `PUT /api/layouts/:id`, `DELETE /api/layouts/:id` and `POST /api/layouts/:id/duplicate`: replace a layout (keeping its id; a 404 for an unknown one, a 409 for a name clash), delete one (204; a 409 naming the display and the schedule if a schedule rule in `config.yaml` shows it) or copy it as "Copy of …" (201). Each write sends `layouts.updated` to every socket.
- `GET /api/photos/next?display=<id>&orientation=landscape|portrait`: moves that display on to its next photo, rendered for the shape of its stage (`orientation` is landscape if left out; anything else is a 400), and answers `{ "photo": { "id", "url", "width", "height", "orientation", "taken_at", "provider" }, "interval_seconds" }`, or `{ "photo": null }` when there is nothing to show (no `photos` block, an empty or unreachable source with nothing cached). `interval_seconds` is how long the server leaves a photo on show, as it is set now (the settings page can change it), which the photo frame paces its slow pan and zoom by; it is left out when there is no `photos` block. `url` is where to load the image from, `width`, `height` and `orientation` (`landscape` or `portrait`) are those of the original photo once turned upright, `taken_at` is an ISO 8601 time or `null`, and `provider` is `immich`, `google` or `local`. The photo comes from the display's `photo_source` if that source is set up in the config, otherwise from the first that is (Immich, then Google Photos, then the folder). Each display has its own shuffled playlist per source, kept in SQLite: every photo is shown once before any repeats, and a new round never starts with the photo just shown. It is a 400 without a `display`, and a 404 for one that isn't in the config. Never cached, and open like every GET
- `GET /photos/:name`: a rendered photo, by the name in a photo's `url` (`<sha256>.webp`). It is `image/webp` with `Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable` and `X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff`. A name of any other shape, or a file that isn't there, is a 404. It is not under `/api` and needs no PIN, because the displays load it as an ordinary image
- `GET /api/displays`: every display's settings from the config, with any preferences saved on the Displays tab on top (`id`, `name`, `theme`, `month_style`, `week_start`, `burn_in`, `motion`, `takeover_art`, `night_text`, `orientation`, `week_hours`, `day_parts`, `weather_on_dates`, `show_locations`, the config's `timezone` and `photo_sources`, the photo sources that are set up, in the order a display falls back to them (`immich`, `google`, `local`; empty with no `photos` block, and `google` never for now), which the remote uses to disable the others), which the display page uses for its theme and time zone
- `GET /api/displays/:id/state`: the display's current state (`view`, `month`, `month_set_at`, `week`, `week_set_at`, `screen`, `photo_source`, `last_calendar_view`, `brightness`, `auto_presence`, `auto_brightness`, `schedules_off`, `schedule`, `overlay`, `camera`), or a 404 for an id that isn't in the config
- `POST /api/displays/:id/commands`: applies one command, such as `{ "type": "set_view", "view": "agenda" }`, and answers with the new state. The types are `set_view`, `next_view`, `set_month` (`YYYY-MM`, `current`, `prev` or `next`), `set_week` (a `YYYY-MM-DD` date in the wanted week, or `current`, `prev` or `next`; it shows the week view if another view is up, and stays on the week by part of day view, the week and photo view or a custom layout with a week if one of those is up), `set_layout` (`{ "layout": "<id>" }`, which shows that custom layout; an id with no layout is a 404), `set_screen`, `set_brightness`, `set_photo_source`, `set_auto_presence` (`{ "auto_presence": true | false }`), `set_auto_brightness` (the same with `auto_brightness`), `set_schedule` (`{ "schedule": "<id>", "on": true | false }`, which turns one of the display's schedules on or off; an id it doesn't have is a 404), `set_overlay` (`{ "text", "icon"?, "ttl"? }` in seconds, or `null` to clear), `set_camera` (`{ "camera": "<id>", "seconds"?, "wake"? }`, which shows one of the config's `cameras` over the view for `seconds`, 5 to 1800, default 60, and with `wake` (the default) turns a screen that is off on and off again when the camera goes; `{ "camera": null }` takes it down, and a camera the config doesn't have is a 404; it is not an MQTT command), `photo_next`, `toggle_photo` (the photo frame if it is not on, otherwise the view it replaced, which the state keeps as `last_calendar_view`; the button's double press, not an MQTT topic), `refresh` and `preview_special_day` (`{ "id" }` for a special day or `{ "scene" }` for a scene, exactly one, and `"seconds"` from 5 to 600, default 60: the wall shows it as if it were today until `state.preview.until`, and the server then clears it by itself; it is not an MQTT command). A bad command is a 400 with a short reason, and a `preview_special_day` of a day or scene that doesn't exist is a 404. With `remote.pin` set it needs the `X-Wallcal-Pin` header, as `POST /api/refresh` does
- `GET /api/remote`: `{ "pin_required": true | false }`, whether writes need a PIN. It never returns the PIN
- `POST /api/remote/pin`: answers 204 if the `X-Wallcal-Pin` header holds the right PIN (or none is set), and 401 or 429 as above. The remote page uses it to check a PIN as it is typed in
- `GET /api/stars`: the star chart, `{ "people": [ { "id", "name", "initial", "colour", "period", "goal" } ], "counts": { "<person id>": { "YYYY-MM-DD": <stars> } } }`: the people who have a chart switched on, in config order, and each one's stars for the last 14 days up to today in the config's time zone (a day without stars is left out)
- `POST /api/stars/:personId`: adds stars to a person's day, or with a negative `delta` takes some away, and answers with the whole new chart. The body is `{ "delta": <whole number from -10 to 10, not 0>, "date"?: "YYYY-MM-DD" }`, and `date` is today if it is left out. A count stops at 0 and at 99. An unknown person is a 404, a person whose chart is switched off a 409, a bad body a 400 that names the field, and a `date` in the future or more than 13 days before today a 400. With `remote.pin` set, it needs the `X-Wallcal-Pin` header
- `GET /api/settings/stars`: every person in the config, in config order, with their star chart settings: `{ "id", "name", "initial", "colour", "enabled", "period": "day" | "week", "goal": <1 to 99> | null }`. A person who has never been set up is off, counts a week and has no goal
- `PUT /api/settings/stars/:personId`: replaces a person's settings with the body `{ "enabled", "period", "goal" }` (all three are needed) and answers with their entry as above. An unknown person is a 404 and a bad body a 400 that names the field. It needs the PIN, like any write
- `GET /api/settings/calendars`: one entry per calendar, in config order, for the settings page: its default person, its `icon` (or null), whether detection is on, source type, `poll_seconds`, the last attempt, last success and last error (a short message, never a feed URL), the stored event count, and `fallbacks`, the recent events (30 days either way, at most 20) that no name, alias or rule matched, so they fell back to the default person. A recurring event is one entry with an `occurrences` count. `fallbacks` is empty when `detect_people` is `false`
- `GET /api/settings/displays`: one entry per display: its settings, current state, the open WebSocket connections by role (`display` and `remote`), and the MQTT connection (`off`, `connecting`, `connected` or `disconnected`, with the last error, which holds no secret)
- `GET /api/settings/connections`: whether MQTT is set up (the broker as scheme, host and port only, whether discovery is on, and how many displays are connected) and whether Home Assistant answers with its token (`ok`, `error` with a short reason, or `off`, and when it was last checked). Home Assistant is asked at most once a minute, however often the page polls
- `GET /api/settings/photos`: the Photos tab's data: `{ "configured": false }` with no `photos` block, otherwise `{ "configured": true, "interval_seconds", "match_orientation", "cache_bytes", "cache_max_bytes", "providers": [{ "id", "mode", "where", "photos", "cached", "last_success_at", "last_error" }], "google_ignored" }`. `where` is Immich's host and port or the local folder's path, `mode` is Immich's mode (`null` for the folder), and `last_error` is a short message. The API key, the Immich address beyond its host, and the `google` secrets are never in it
- `POST /api/settings/photos/refresh`: lists every photo source again now and answers with the same shape as the GET above. It is a 409 with a short `message` when there is no `photos` block. A write, so it needs the PIN
- `POST /api/settings/discovery`: publishes the Home Assistant discovery configs again for every display that is connected to the broker, and answers `{ "republished": ["hallway"] }`. It is a 409 with a short `message` when there is no `mqtt` block or `discovery` is off
- `GET /api/settings/preferences`: the Displays tab's preferences: `{ "displays": [{ "id", "name", "values", "config", "changed" }], "shared": { "values", "config", "changed" }, "photos_configured", "alarm_configured", "mqtt_configured" }`. `values` is what is in force, `config` is what `config.yaml` says, and `changed` lists the keys with a saved change. A display's keys are `theme`, `month_style`, `week_start`, `month_return_minutes`, `week_hours`, `day_parts`, `weather_on_dates` (`{ "month", "week", "week_parts", "agenda" }`, each `true` or `false`: which views draw the day's forecast next to their dates), `show_locations` (`true` or `false`: whether the views show an event's location under its title where there is room), `burn_in`, `motion` (`off` or `subtle`: slow ambient motion), `takeover_art` (`behind`, `over_in_portrait` or `over`: where a special day's takeover art is drawn), `night_text` and `orientation`; the shared keys are `photo_interval_seconds`, `off_after_minutes`, `quiet_hours` (`{ "from", "to" }` or `null`), `night_action`, `night_below_lux`, `night_above_lux` (`null` for twice the dark threshold), `min_step` and `smoothing_seconds`
- `PUT /api/settings/preferences/displays/:id` and `PUT /api/settings/preferences/shared`: save preferences, as `{ "set": { "theme": "dark" }, "reset": ["week_start"] }` (`reset` goes back to the config's value), and answer with the same shape as the GET. A refused value is a 400 whose `data.fields` has a message for each key (nothing is saved), and an unknown display is a 404. A change is in force at once: the display's sockets get `settings.updated`, and the month return timer, the photo rotation and the policies pick it up. Writes, so they need the PIN

None of the settings routes ever returns a secret: no feed URL, Home Assistant URL or token, MQTT username or password, or PIN.

**The PIN.** With `remote.pin` set, every write under `/api` needs it in an `X-Wallcal-Pin` header: any method but GET, HEAD or OPTIONS, for the routes above and for any added later. A missing or wrong PIN is a 401, and an address that sends 10 wrong ones in 5 minutes gets a 429 until the window ends. The check runs before the route, so an unknown display id or a bad body gets a 401 too, not a 404 or 400. Reading, and the `/_ws` WebSocket, stay open.
- `GET /remote.webmanifest?id=<display>`: the web app manifest for "Add to Home Screen" (`<display name> remote`, opening `/remote?id=<display>`). `id` defaults to the first display; an unknown one is a 404. Its icons are in `calendar-app/public/icons/`, and `node scripts/render-icons.mjs` redraws them. `GET /display.webmanifest?id=<display>` is the same for the display page (`<display name> wall`, scoped to `/display`, fullscreen with a standalone fallback, the theme's background as its colour), with `display-*` icons beside the remote's and the app-wide favicon, and `/display-sw.js` is the display's service worker
- `/_ws?display=<id>&role=display|remote`: a WebSocket that sends the display's whole state when it connects, then the weather (`weather.updated`, with `weather: null` when there is none), the special days (`special_days.updated`), the custom layouts (`layouts.updated`, the whole library, as `{ "type": "layouts.updated", "layouts": [ … ] }`, and again whenever it changes) the next alarm (`alarm.updated`, as `{ "type": "alarm.updated", "alarm": "2026-10-31T06:45:00.000Z" | null }`), the display's kitchen timers (`timers.updated`, as `{ "type": "timers.updated", "display_id", "timers": [ … ] }`, an empty list when there are none, and again whenever they change) and the star chart (`stars.updated`, as `{ "type": "stars.updated", "stars": { … } }` with the same chart as `GET /api/stars`, and again whenever a person's settings or stars change), and the display's settings (`settings.updated`, as `{ "type": "settings.updated", "display_id", "settings" }` with the same settings as `GET /api/displays`), then `state.updated`, `events.updated`, `weather.updated`, `special_days.updated`, `alarm.updated`, `timers.updated`, `stars.updated` and `settings.updated` as they happen (and `photo.next` to the display only, as `{ "type": "photo.next", "display_id" }`: the cue to show another photo, sent every `photos.interval_seconds` while the display is on the `photo` view, the `week_photo` view or a custom layout with a photo pane, at once for a `photo_next` command, and never to a remote). Commands are sent with the POST above, not over the socket

### Configuration

`pnpm dev` reads `calendar-app/config.dev.yaml`, so it needs no setup. A production build reads `/config/config.yaml`, and `secrets.yaml` from the same folder. [`config.example.yaml`](config.example.yaml) and [`secrets.example.yaml`](secrets.example.yaml) show every option. A bad config stops the server at start-up with a message listing each problem.

In the config, `key: !secret some_name` takes its value from `some_name` in `secrets.yaml`. The environment variable `WALLCAL_SECRET_SOME_NAME` overrides it.

| Variable | Effect |
|---|---|
| `WALLCAL_CONFIG_FILE` | Full path to the config file. |
| `WALLCAL_CONFIG_DIR` | Folder holding `config.yaml` and `secrets.yaml`. Default `/config`. |
| `WALLCAL_SECRET_<NAME>` | Overrides the secret `<name>` (capitals, with each character that isn't a letter or digit turned into `_`). |
| `WALLCAL_TIMEZONE` | Overrides `timezone` in the config. |
| `WALLCAL_DATA_DIR` | Folder for the database and other app data. Default `/data` (`.data/` under `pnpm dev`). |
| `WALLCAL_MIGRATIONS_DIR` | Folder holding the SQL migrations. Normally not needed. |

Events are kept for a rolling window of whole months around the current one: 12 months back and 12 ahead unless `sync.months_back` and `sync.months_ahead` say otherwise. Recurring events are expanded into single events inside it. The month picker on the remote reaches 12 months either side, so a smaller window leaves the months beyond it empty. Calendar sources are synced by the library in `calendar-app/server/sources/` (ICS and Home Assistant).

Each calendar is polled on its own interval (`poll_seconds`, 120 for ICS by default), and the first polls are spread over a few seconds at start-up. A feed that fails keeps its last good events and is tried again after twice the interval, then four times, and so on up to an hour; the first success after that goes back to the normal interval. The reason for the last failure is stored in `sync_status` and logged, without the feed's URL, which is a secret. `POST /api/refresh` forces an immediate poll of every calendar.

A `homeassistant` calendar reads `GET /api/calendars/<entity_id>` over the same window, with the long-lived access token from the `homeassistant` block (create one under your profile in Home Assistant, in Security, Long-lived access tokens). It is polled every 300 seconds unless `poll_seconds` says otherwise. Home Assistant expands recurring events itself, and an all-day event stays all-day. Home Assistant has no ETag, so each poll reads the whole window, but only the rows that changed are written. A failure backs off and keeps the last good events, as with ICS, and the token is never logged or stored in `sync_status`.

A `weather` block names a Home Assistant `weather` entity, which is read through the same `homeassistant` block: its state for the current conditions and `POST /api/services/weather/get_forecasts?return_response` for the daily forecast, so the entity must support daily forecasts. The hourly forecast is asked for too, and is optional: an entity without one (HTTP 400) just leaves out the photo frame's "Today's forecast", which is built from it. It is polled every 600 seconds unless `poll_seconds` says otherwise (at least 60), with the same backoff as a calendar, and the last good weather is kept while Home Assistant fails. A change is sent to every display as `weather.updated`. Leave the block out and the views draw no weather. See [Weather in docs/home-assistant.md](docs/home-assistant.md#weather).

An `alarm` block names the Home Assistant entity that holds the next alarm, for a display whose `night_text` is `next_alarm` (the night view shows it under its clock; `first_event` shows the next timed event instead, and the default `none` shows only the clock). It is a `sensor` with a timestamp state, such as the companion app's next alarm sensor, or an `input_datetime` that has a date, read with `GET /api/states/<entity_id>` through the `homeassistant` block. It is polled every 300 seconds unless `poll_seconds` says otherwise (at least 30), with the same backoff as the weather, and the last good answer is kept while Home Assistant fails. A state that is not a time (`unknown`, `unavailable`, empty) means no alarm. A change is sent to every display as `alarm.updated`. See [Next alarm in docs/home-assistant.md](docs/home-assistant.md#next-alarm).

A `timers` block lists Home Assistant entities that hold kitchen timers, so the wall can show countdowns: a Nest speaker's `sensor.<speaker>_timers` from the [ha-google-home](https://github.com/leikoilja/ha-google-home) integration, or a `timer.*` helper. Each entity can be limited to some `displays` (every display if left out). The `homeassistant` block is needed. Each is read with `GET /api/states/<entity_id>` every `poll_seconds` (30 by default, at least 5), with the same backoff as the alarm, and a second WebSocket connection to Home Assistant, which needs an administrator's token, sends changes as they happen. A running timer that disappears at its end is kept for `done_seconds` (60 by default, 0 to 600) as done; one that disappears earlier was cancelled. A display's changes are sent as `timers.updated`. See [Kitchen timers in docs/home-assistant.md](docs/home-assistant.md#kitchen-timers).

`cameras` and `triggers` let Home Assistant put a camera on the wall, such as the front door when the doorbell rings. A camera is an `id`, a `name` and the `url` of a feed the wall's browser loads itself, such as one from go2rtc (`stream.html`, `api/stream.mp4` or `api/stream.mjpeg`); `kind` (`page`, `image` or `video`) says how to show it, and is worked out from the address when left out. A trigger is something in Home Assistant to listen for (`when`: an `entity_id` changing state, with optional `from` and `to`, or an `event_type` on its bus) and the `actions` to apply to its `displays` (every display if left out) when it happens. An action is any command `POST /api/displays/:id/commands` takes, so a trigger can show a camera (`set_camera`), a message, a view or a layout. The app subscribes to the triggers over Home Assistant's WebSocket API with the `homeassistant` block's token, which must be an administrator's, and connects again by itself if Home Assistant goes away. [docs/home-assistant.md](docs/home-assistant.md#doorbell-camera) has a full example.

A `photos` block turns on the photo frame. While a display's view is `photo`, `week_photo` or a custom layout with a photo pane, the server sends it `photo.next` every `interval_seconds` (at least 5, default 60), so the remote, Home Assistant and the button all skip with one `photo_next` command: it sends one at once and starts the wait again, so a skip is never followed straight away by a scheduled change. Going from one of them to another leaves the timer running, and leaving them all stops that display's timer, and with no `photos` block there is none. `match_orientation: true` shows each display only photos of its own shape: portrait photos are left out on a landscape display and landscape ones on a portrait display, and a photo passed over keeps its place in the playlist for a display of the other shape (`landscape_only: true` is the older name for it, and means the same). Every photo is rendered on the server to a WebP of the shape of the display's stage, 2560×1440 for a landscape display and 1440×2560 for a portrait one, so the Pi only decodes one image: a photo within about 10% of the stage's shape (16:9 or 9:16) is cropped to fill the screen, and any other shape is fitted whole over a blurred, enlarged and slightly darkened copy of itself. EXIF orientation is applied first. A photo is rendered for a shape the first time a display of that shape shows it, so a photo can have one file in the cache for each shape. Rendered photos are kept in `photos/` in the data folder, named after a hash of their contents, up to `cache_max_mb` (2048 by default). Over the cap, the photos shown longest ago are deleted first, never one a display is showing or is about to show. The playlist survives a restart. Each source's list of photos is read at start-up and every 10 minutes, in the background, so a slow or missing source never delays the start. A photo is fetched and rendered when it is first due, and the one after it is prepared ahead; if a source can't be reached, the photos already in the cache keep rotating, and a source that was never reachable just has nothing to show. Two sources are built: Immich and a local folder (the `google` block is accepted in the config but ignored for now, with a warning in the log). A display uses the source in its `photo_source` if it is set up, otherwise the first that is (Immich, then the folder).

A display can have `schedules`: rules such as "agenda 06:30–09:00" or "photo frame 10:00–16:00 on weekdays", each with a view (a `custom` rule also names the `layout` to show, by its id, and any rule with photos can name a `photo_feed` to show for its window, which is shown even when the wall is already on that view), `from` and `to` times and `days` (`every_day`, `weekdays`, `weekends` or a list such as `[mon, wed]`). Times are in the config's `timezone`, and a window may run past midnight. The app puts the view on the wall at the start and goes back to the view from before it at the end, and acts at nothing else, so a view chosen by hand lasts until the next start or end. Each schedule has a switch in Home Assistant to turn it off. They work without MQTT; see `config.example.yaml` and `docs/architecture.md` §7.8.

The photo frame is controlled from the remote (the Photo frame and Week + photo tiles, the "Photos from" control and Next photo), from Home Assistant (the View and Photo source selects and the Next photo button) and from the button. `toggle_photo` switches between the photo frame and the view it replaced (`last_calendar_view`: the last view that wasn't the photo frame, `month` until there is one). `next_view` goes round the views and skips `week_photo` when the config has no photo source, since its photo pane would be empty. The `button` block's gestures can be `next_view`, `toggle_photo`, `next_photo`, `toggle_screen`, `next_month`, `prev_month`, `this_month`, `next_week`, `prev_week`, `this_week`, `refresh`, `ha` or `none`; `server/control/button.ts` turns each into its command, and the app applies it when the Pi agent publishes the press on `pi/button`.

`immich` takes `url`, `api_key` and a `mode`. The key is sent in the `x-api-key` header (make one in Immich under Account Settings, API Keys: a key with all permissions works, and a limited one needs to read assets, albums and memories and to view thumbnails) and is never logged or put in an error message. `url` is the address of Immich, which may have a path if it is behind a reverse proxy, and must not hold a user name or password. Redirects are not followed, so a redirect from `http` to `https` is reported with a message that says to use the address it points to. Only the display-sized `thumbnail?size=preview` of a photo is downloaded, never the original, and a photo is fetched when it is first due, not when it is listed. Only photos (not videos) are used, and photos in the bin or archived are left out. The size comes from the photo's EXIF (turned if it was saved on its side) so that `landscape_only` works before a photo is downloaded, and the date from its EXIF date taken. The three modes:

- `albums`: every photo in each of the albums in `album_ids`, read with `POST /api/search/metadata` a page of 1000 at a time, and a photo that is in two albums once. It is the default when `album_ids` has something in it.
- `random`: a random pick of `random.count` photos (200 by default, at most 1000) from the whole library with `POST /api/search/random`, optionally only those of the people in `random.person_ids` or between `random.taken_after` and `random.taken_before` (a day such as `"2024-12-31"`, or a time with a zone; a bare day is in UTC and `taken_before` includes the whole of it). It is the default when there are no album ids. A new pick is made at every listing (every 10 minutes), so the pool changes under the playlist: the photos that were shown lately (up to 100) are kept in it as well, which keeps them in the cache and gives the frame more to rotate if Immich is down, and a photo still on the wall is never taken away.
- `memories`: the photos in today's "on this day" memories (`GET /api/memories`). On a day with none, it uses `random` (with the same `random` settings) so the frame is never empty, and goes back to memories once there are some. The memories are read again at every listing, so they change at midnight, not at once.

If Immich can't be reached the photos already in the cache keep rotating. The log says why once (and again when the reason changes): "Immich refused the API key" for a 401 or 403, "Immich could not be reached" for a connection that fails, times out (30 seconds) or answers with an error. A listing that fails leaves the pool as it was.

`local.path` is a folder of pictures (JPEG, PNG, WebP, AVIF, GIF and TIFF, in sub-folders too; hidden files and folders are left out; a photo's date is read from its EXIF when it has one). An absolute path, or one relative to the folder holding the config file, as for `file:` calendar URLs. A folder that is missing is a warning in the log, not an error, and is read again every 10 minutes. In Docker, mount it into the container (for example `./photos:/photos:ro`, with `path: /photos`). `config.dev.yaml` points at the few small pictures in `calendar-app/test/fixtures/photos/` (made by `scripts/make-photo-fixtures.mjs`).

`unsplash` is a fourth source, free photography from [Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/developers): make an application there and give its access key as `access_key` (it is sent in the `Authorization: Client-ID` header to `api.unsplash.com` only, and is never logged). The pool is one of `query` (a search, `landscape` if nothing is set), `collections` (collection ids) or `topics` (topic slugs), narrowed by `orientation` and `content_filter`, `count` photos long (at most 300) and listed again every `refresh_minutes` (60 by default; 10 to 10080, as the photo service wakes every 10 minutes). With `pick: random` (the default) each refresh asks Unsplash for new random photos, so the pool changes every time, and the photos shown lately stay in it; with `pick: fixed` each refresh reads the same search, collections or topics again, which gives the same photos until they change on Unsplash. A demo key may make only 50 requests an hour: listing costs one per 30 photos (count 60 every 60 minutes is 2 an hour), and Unsplash also asks to be told about each new photo shown, which is skipped while fewer than 10 requests are left in the hour so the listings keep their budget. Photos are shown from Unsplash's own image address, rendered and cached like any other, and each is shown with its credit ("Photo by Jane Doe on Unsplash") in small type in the bottom right corner of the photo, as Unsplash's guidelines ask.

A **photo feed** is a named pool of photos from one source with its own query, so a layout or a time of day can have its own kind of photo. Each source block you set up (`immich`, `local`, `unsplash`) is a feed whose id is the source's name, and `photos.feeds` adds more: each has an `id` (lower case letters, digits, `-` and `_`, and not a source's name), an optional `name`, and one of `immich`, `local` or `unsplash` with just the settings that differ, such as `unsplash: { query: "mountain lake" }`. A feed borrows the key, address or folder it needs from the top-level block of its source, so that block must be there. See `config.example.yaml`.

Which feed a display shows is decided at each photo, most specific first: the schedule window in charge (a rule's `photo_feed`), then the custom layout on the wall (its **Photos from** choice, `photo_feed`), then the display's own choice (the remote's and Home Assistant's Photos from, `photo_source`), then the first source that is set up. A name that is no longer set up is skipped, so a feed taken out of the config leaves the frame showing the next choice rather than nothing. When the choice changes while a photo view stays up (a schedule window with a feed begins or ends, or the layout on the wall is edited to another feed) the photo moves on at once.

Who an event is for (its people) and the title shown on the wall are worked out when it is stored, from the names in its title and description and the calendar's keyword rules, as described in [architecture §3.3](docs/architecture.md). If the people, groups or rules in the config change, the stored events are re-resolved at the next start without fetching anything, and a calendar that has been removed from the config is deleted along with its events.

To hide an event from the wall, put a line that is just `hide` in its description (case-insensitive; `hide this` or `hidden` won't do). It is left out of every view but stays stored, so removing the line brings it back at the next sync.

### Custom layouts

A custom layout puts several views on the wall at once: the month, the week, the week by part of day, the agenda, the photo frame, the day grid and the star chart, arranged into rows and panes, such as a photo beside the week above the agenda. Layouts are a library shared by every display, kept in the database (not in `config.yaml`) and edited on the **Layouts** tab of the settings page or from the **Edit layouts** sheet on the remote. Each layout has two arrangements, one for a landscape wall and one for a portrait wall, which you edit separately; a new layout's portrait arrangement starts as the landscape one turned on its side, and **Start again from landscape** does that again. Both arrangements hold the same views, each once. The **day grid** is a rows by columns grid of consecutive day tiles with their events (1 to 6 rows, 1 to 7 columns, starting today or at the start of this week), which has its own size in each arrangement; it always starts from the real today, not the pinned month or week, and a layout of just the day grid is a full-screen one. The **star chart** is a rows by columns grid of cards, one for each person who has a chart switched on (1 to 6 rows, 1 to 6 columns, which it has in each arrangement), each with the person's stars for the day or the week against their goal, as star icons or a progress bar, and the days of the week as a strip when the card is tall enough; it has a Title option, like the month's, for the header above the cards. It is a layout component only, like the day grid: there is no view or remote tile for it, and MQTT and Home Assistant know nothing of it. See Star chart, below. An arrangement is up to 4 rows of up to 4 panes, and a row's or pane's size is a weight from 1 to 4 against its neighbours.

The views in the panes are the ordinary ones, so a pane smaller than a view was designed for shows it scaled down rather than clipped. The photo pane needs a photo source, and **Photos from**, beside its Display options, chooses which photo feed it draws from (a source such as Unsplash, or one of the `photos.feeds`), or leaves it to the display's own choice; a layout without a photo pane keeps no feed, a feed that is no longer in the config shows as "not set up" until you choose another, and a save that names one that is not set up is refused with the ones that are. Duplicating a layout copies its feed. A special day's takeover reaches the calendar panes but not the photo. The photo pane's **Display** options choose its overlays: the time, the date, the weather, and **Today's forecast** (off unless you turn it on), each with a corner where it applies (the date's and the forecast's corners, and the time's, which the weather shares), or the corner that moves round against burn-in. Today's forecast is a row of columns in the pill, one for each part of the day still to come (morning 06:00 to 11:00, noon 11:00 to 14:00, afternoon 14:00 to 18:00, evening 18:00 to 22:00), each with the expected condition's icon, the temperature and the rain (the chance in per cent when Home Assistant gives one, else the amount in mm); after 22:00 it shows tomorrow's parts under a "Tomorrow" heading. It needs the hourly forecast from the weather entity (the `weather` block of the configuration), and is left out without it.

To show a layout, tap its tile on the remote, choose it in Home Assistant's Layout select, send `set_layout` (`POST /api/displays/:id/commands`) or `layout/set` (MQTT), let `next_view` or the button step on to it, or name it in a schedule rule (`view: custom`, `layout: <id>`; the id is shown in the layout's editor). A layout cannot be deleted while a schedule names it. Deleting one that is on the wall puts the month view up in its place. See `docs/architecture.md` §4.8.

### Star chart

A star chart rewards people for the stars they earn. It is off for everyone to start with. On the **Stars** tab of the settings page, switch it on for a person and choose what it counts: a **day** or a **week** (from the display's `week_start`), and an optional goal of 1 to 99 stars for that period. The same tab lists this week's stars for everyone who has a chart, with a minus and a plus on each day, to put a miscount right (days from the last two weeks can be changed). Stars are normally given from the Stars section of the remote, with a plus and a minus for each person, always for today.

The chart is drawn by the `stars` component of a custom layout, so a layout decides where it goes and how big it is: add the Stars chip in the layouts editor and set its **Rows** and **Columns** (1 to 6 each, in each arrangement; a strip under another pane is 1×3, a block 2×2). A card is shown for each person with a chart, in the order of `people` in the config, left to right and then top to bottom; people beyond the grid are left out, and a tile with no person is left blank. A card has the person's initial and name, their total against their goal (`7 / 10`, with "today" or "this week"), a row of stars (filled for those earned, outlined for the rest of the goal, and `+N` for any beyond it or beyond what fits), or a progress bar for a goal of more than 10, and, when the card is tall enough, the seven days of the week with each day's stars. A person whose goal is reached has a ring and a faint wash of their colour. A **Title** option on the pane shows or hides a header with "Stars" and the week. A pane with more cards than fit at a readable size is drawn larger and scaled down, like the day grid. The wall is sent the last 14 days of stars (`stars.updated`) and keeps them for when the server can't be reached. Stars are stored in SQLite, by person and day, so they stay when a person is switched off or removed from the config. See `docs/architecture.md` §4.8b.

### MQTT

With an `mqtt` block in the config, each display keeps its state on an MQTT broker and takes commands from it. [`contracts/mqtt.json`](contracts/mqtt.json) is the reference: every topic, its direction, whether it is retained, and a JSON Schema for its payload. Everything lives under `wallcal/<display id>/`, as text:

- The app publishes the state, retained: `view/state`, `month/state` (the month the month view shows, as `YYYY-MM`), `week/state` (the first day of the week the week view shows, as `YYYY-MM-DD`), `screen/state`, `photo_source/state`, `brightness/state`, `auto_presence/state` and `auto_brightness/state` (`ON` or `OFF`), `schedule/<schedule_id>/state` (`ON` or `OFF`, one for each schedule in the display's config), `layout/state` (the name of the custom layout the display is set to show, or empty when there is none; it stays while the view is something else) and `overlay/state` (the text of the message shown over the display, or empty once it has expired or been cleared). A change is published when it happens, and everything is published again whenever the connection to the broker is made. `last_sync/state` is when the last calendar sync succeeded, as a UTC time like `2026-10-28T12:00:00.000Z`. It is not published until a calendar has synced. `special_day/state` is the name of today's special day, or empty when there is none, and changes at midnight in the display time zone.
- The app subscribes to `view/set`, `month/set`, `week/set`, `screen/set`, `photo_source/set`, `brightness/set`, `auto_presence/set`, `auto_brightness/set`, `schedule/<schedule_id>/set`, `layout/set` (a layout's id or exact name), `overlay/set`, `photo/next` and `refresh`. `view/set` takes `custom` too, and `next` then goes on through the layouts. A payload that doesn't fit its topic is ignored and logged. A retained message on one of these topics is ignored too, so it isn't replayed at every reconnect; publish an empty retained message there to remove it from the broker.
- `availability` is `online` while the app is connected. The broker publishes `offline` (the last will) if the app dies, and the app publishes it itself on a clean stop. The app opens one connection per display, named `<client_id>-<display id>`, so that each display has its own last will.

The topics that the Raspberry Pi uses (`pi/…`) are in the contract too. The [Pi agent](#pi-agent) publishes `pi/availability`, `pi/cpu_temperature`, `pi/uptime`, `pi/screen/state`, `pi/button` (a single, double or long press of the physical button, not retained), `pi/presence` (`ON` or `OFF`, retained: whether the mmWave sensor sees someone) and `pi/illuminance` (the light in lux as text, not retained: sent when it changes meaningfully and at least every 30 seconds). The app publishes one: `pi/screen/set`, retained, which is the display's `screen` state (`on` or `off`), sent whenever it changes and again on every connect, so the agent can turn the panel on or off. The app subscribes to `pi/button` and does what the `button:` block says for the gesture (see `button` in `config.example.yaml`); a retained message there is ignored, like on the command topics. It subscribes to `pi/presence` too, for the auto presence policy: presence turns the screen on, and nobody for `off_after_minutes` turns it off (see `policies` in `config.example.yaml`). Here the retained value is accepted, because that is how the app learns the current state when it connects. It subscribes to `pi/illuminance` for the auto brightness policy: the brightness follows the light along a curve, with the readings smoothed and a minimum step so it doesn't creep, and in a dark room it can switch to the night view or turn the screen off (see `auto_brightness` under `policies` in `config.example.yaml`). A retained message there is ignored, because a stale reading is worse than none. It reads no other `pi/` topic. The app runs the same without a broker, or while the broker is down: it keeps retrying in the background, with a growing wait of up to a minute, and logs the first failure rather than every attempt.

To watch it and to send a command, with the Mosquitto tools:

```sh
mosquitto_sub -h localhost -t 'wallcal/#' -v
mosquitto_pub -h localhost -t wallcal/hallway/view/set -m agenda
```

#### Home Assistant

The app also publishes MQTT discovery configs, so Home Assistant creates a "Wall Calendar" device for each display with no YAML. With more than one display the devices are named "Wall Calendar Hallway" and so on. The device has a view select, a "Month shown" sensor, previous, next and this month buttons, a "Week shown" sensor, previous, next and this week buttons, a screen switch, a photo source select, a Layout select (its options are the custom layouts' names, and it is left out while there are none), a brightness slider, auto presence and auto brightness switches, a switch for each schedule, a message text box (it shows a banner on the wall; clear the box to remove it, and it empties by itself when the message runs out or is cleared elsewhere), next photo and refresh buttons, a "Last sync" sensor, and a "Special day" sensor (the name of today's special day, or empty).

The configs are retained, so it makes no difference whether the app or Home Assistant starts first. If Home Assistant restarts, it announces `online` on `homeassistant/status`, and the app publishes the configs again. The entities show as unavailable while the app is offline (they follow `availability`). If Home Assistant listens on a different discovery prefix, set `discovery_prefix` under `mqtt`. Set `discovery: false` to turn all of this off.

To remove a device, delete it in Home Assistant, and set `discovery: false` or the app will bring it back at the next start. Or publish an empty retained message on each `homeassistant/<type>/wallcal_<display id>/<entity>/config` topic.

[docs/home-assistant.md](docs/home-assistant.md) covers broker setup, example automations and a dashboard card.

### Database

Events, sync status, display state, photo records, special days and star charts are stored in SQLite (`better-sqlite3`, with Drizzle ORM). The database is `wallcal.db` in the data folder, which is `/data` in production and `calendar-app/.data/` under `pnpm dev` (git-ignored). Set `WALLCAL_DATA_DIR` to use another folder. The folder is created if it is missing, and the server stops at start-up with a message if it can't open the database there.

Migrations run at start-up, so there is nothing to do by hand. After changing `server/db/schema.ts`, run `pnpm db:generate` and commit the new files in `server/db/migrations/`. CI fails if the schema and the migrations disagree. The build copies the migrations to `.output/server/db/migrations`, and `WALLCAL_MIGRATIONS_DIR` can point the server at a different folder.

Checks, all run from `calendar-app/` and all run in CI:

```sh
pnpm lint
pnpm typecheck
pnpm test
pnpm build
```

`pnpm test` builds the app once, before the first of the e2e tests (`test/e2e`), and they all start that build. CI builds first and sets `WALLCAL_E2E_PREBUILT=1`, which tells the tests to use the build that is already in `.output`; do the same by hand to run them again without a rebuild.

The visual tests (`display.spec.ts` for the shell, `agenda.spec.ts` for the agenda view, `month.spec.ts` for the month view and `week-parts.spec.ts` for the week by part of day, `locations.spec.ts` for event locations in each of the views (with and without room, and never a changed "+N more"), `photo.spec.ts` for the photo frame, `week-photo.spec.ts` for the week and photo view, `custom-layout.spec.ts` for custom layouts, including the day grid and the star chart, and `layouts-editor.spec.ts` for the layouts editor, whose portrait pictures are in `portrait.spec.ts`) are Playwright screenshots at 2560×1440 with a frozen clock and fixture events, in both themes. `remote.spec.ts` does the same for the phone remote at 390×844, in the phone's light and dark settings, with the screen off, with a pinned month, and with the photo frame on, and with a Stars section, and checks that a tap sends the right command. They run against the built app, so build first:

```sh
pnpm build
pnpm test:visual          # compare with the baselines in test/visual/*-snapshots
pnpm test:visual:update   # refresh the baselines after a change to how the display looks
```

Playwright is pinned to an exact version in `package.json` because the baselines are only valid for the Chromium that goes with it. Make them with that Chromium (CI installs the same one with `playwright install chromium`) and look at each changed PNG before committing it. A failed run leaves a report in `playwright-report/` and the actual and diff images in `test-results/`; both are git-ignored, and CI uploads them. The `display` job in CI runs these tests.

`pnpm lint:fix` fixes what ESLint can. After `pnpm build`, `node .output/server/index.mjs` runs the production server.

## Running with Docker

The home server runs the app from the `ghcr.io/kallb123/wall-display/calendar-app` image, built from [`calendar-app/Dockerfile`](calendar-app/Dockerfile) for amd64 and arm64 by the [Publish images](.github/workflows/publish.yml) workflow. Every push to `main` publishes `:latest` and `:sha-<short sha>`; a `v1.2.3` tag publishes `:1.2.3` and `:1.2`. It needs Docker with the Compose plugin. From the repo root:

```sh
mkdir config data
cp config.example.yaml config/config.yaml        # then edit it
cp secrets.example.yaml config/secrets.yaml      # then edit it
sudo chown 1000:1000 data                        # the container runs as uid 1000, not root
docker compose up -d
```

Check <http://localhost:8080/api/health> (use the server's address from another machine). It should return `{"status":"ok", ...,"db":"ok"}`. `docker compose ps` also shows `healthy` once the app is up. Logs are in `docker compose logs -f calendar-app`. After a config change, run `docker compose restart calendar-app`.

- `./config` is mounted read-only at `/config`. `./data` is mounted at `/data` and holds the database, the special day art and the rendered photos (`photos/`).
- `docker compose --profile mqtt up -d` also starts a Mosquitto broker on port 1883, with its settings in `mosquitto/config/mosquitto.conf`. It allows anonymous connections, so keep it on your home network (the file explains how to add a password). Leave the profile off if you use the Home Assistant Mosquitto add-on.
- To update, `docker compose pull` then `docker compose up -d`. `git pull` first if `docker-compose.yml` or the examples changed. To hold a version, change `:latest` in `docker-compose.yml` to a release or `sha-` tag.
- GHCR makes a new package private. Either make it public (the package's settings on GitHub, under "Danger Zone") or run `docker login ghcr.io` on the server with a personal access token that has `read:packages`.
- To build the image from the checkout instead, `docker compose -f docker-compose.yml -f docker-compose.build.yml up -d --build`.
- Back up `./data` and `./config`. Special days and their art, and star charts, are stored only in `./data`.

## Raspberry Pi kiosk

The wall display runs on a Raspberry Pi with Raspberry Pi OS Lite, showing the `/display` page in a Chromium kiosk. [`kiosk/setup.sh`](kiosk/setup.sh) sets that up: auto-login, a minimal Wayland compositor, Chromium with flags tuned for the Pi, no screen blanking, and a restart if Chromium exits. The Pi holds nothing but the display URL, so reflashing is the way to start again.

```sh
sudo ./kiosk/setup.sh --url 'http://<server>:8080/display?id=hallway'
```

For a panel hung in portrait, add `--rotate 90` (or `270`) so the Pi turns its output and the display page lays itself out portrait.

[`kiosk/README.md`](kiosk/README.md) covers reflashing the Pi, the setup options, extra Chromium flags, logs and troubleshooting.

## Hardware probes

[`tools/hw-probe`](tools/hw-probe) is a small tool you copy to the Pi to answer the hardware questions before relying on the answers: whether the monitor can be switched off and on over HDMI-CEC or by turning the HDMI output off, whether DDC/CI brightness works, how smoothly Chromium draws the month grid and photo crossfades at 2560×1440 with the kiosk's flags, and how to tune the button's press timings, the mmWave sensor's range and placement, and the light sensor's thresholds (the sensor probes run the Pi agent's own sensor code). It needs nothing but the Pi's own Python and a few apt packages, and each probe adds its findings to a markdown report, committed as `docs/hardware-findings.md`. [`tools/hw-probe/README.md`](tools/hw-probe/README.md) covers it.

## Pi agent

A small Python service on the Pi that reports to the same MQTT broker as the app. It publishes `pi/availability` (with a last will) and creates a "Wall Calendar Pi" device in Home Assistant with CPU temperature and uptime sensors and a panel power sensor. It follows the display's screen state (`pi/screen/set`) by turning the panel on or off with one of three back ends, chosen in its config: `black` (the default: the wall page goes black and the panel stays on), `cec` (HDMI-CEC standby with `cec-client`) or `output` (the Pi's HDMI output off with `wlopm` or `wlr-randr`, so the monitor goes to standby). The button (GPIO17: single, double and long press) is published on `pi/button`, and as an event entity in Home Assistant. The mmWave presence sensor (an LD2410: its OUT pin on GPIO27 by default, or its UART frames) is published retained on `pi/presence`, and as an occupancy sensor in Home Assistant. The light sensor (a VEML7700 or BH1750 on I²C, which needs I²C switched on in `raspi-config`) is published on `pi/illuminance`, and as an illuminance sensor in Home Assistant. [`pi-agent/install.sh`](pi-agent/install.sh) installs it as a systemd service:

```sh
sudo ./pi-agent/install.sh
sudo nano /etc/wallcal-agent/config.yaml   # display id and MQTT host
sudo systemctl restart wallcal-agent
```

[`pi-agent/README.md`](pi-agent/README.md) covers the config, logs and development.
